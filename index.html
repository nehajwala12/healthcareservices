Part I — The Decision

1 · RECOMMENDATION

✔ PROCEED WITH CONDITIONS — at a first lien of $208–234MM (4.0–4.5× the corrected $52.0MM base), plus a $40MM springing RCF

Priced for the calendar: S+525, 1.00% floor, 98.5 OID, 1% amortization, 75% ECF sweep stepping to 50%/25% below 3.75×/3.00×, minimum liquidity $30MM, and the reporting package at §9a. The top $26MM of the band funds only if the Y1 home-health final rule (publishes before close) lands at −2.5% or better. All four Tier-1 conditions at §10 must be satisfied in full; any one unanswered is a decline at any attachment.

✘ DECLINE — at $300MM and above (5.75×+ through the corrected base; the Y1 trough turns the credit into a covenant negotiation in year one) and at the $360MM unitranche ask as framed (6.9× corrected · FCF negative through year two · going-concern recovery 47–72%)

The decline is arithmetic, not thematic. At the ask, cash interest of ~$34.7MM meets a trough-year EBITDA of $44.8MM that must also fund $9.7MM of capex, taxes, a working-capital build and an $18–24MM one-time stack. The sponsor’s own model (row 44) shows Y1 below its underwrite. Nothing about the asset changes between $234MM and $360MM — only the distance to the first bad November.

Why — the recommendation is built this way

The band is derived, not negotiated. $208MM is 4.0× the corrected base — the level at which the Y1 trough still produces positive FCF after full debt service with the one-time stack equity-funded. $234MM is 4.5× — the level at which the downside case (§6) still amortizes by Y3 under the sweep. The conditionality of the top $26MM exists because a single Federal Register document, published before close, moves the trough by ±$3–4MM (§7.3). Above $300MM no plausible final rule rescues the year-one math, which is why the decline threshold sits there and not at a rounder number.

4.1 De novo economics — credited to the trajectory, not the brochure

The seller’s single largest add-back credits all 14 de novo branches (and 3 recent tuck-ins) at a “mature-state” run-rate of $92K EBITDA per branch per month [SELLER]. The room contains monthly P&Ls for every branch since opening (1.6.2). No cohort is on a path to that number inside the credited window: the 2023 class — the oldest and best — reaches $51K at month 30; the 2024-H1 class tracks ~10% below it at the same age; the 2024-H2 class ~15% below that. We credit each branch to its own cohort curve, time-stamped, which is worth $3.2MM today [WINDROSE VIEW], with a further $2.7MM held contingent at §4.5 if the 2024-H2 class holds its curve through month 30 (verifiable pre-close for two of four branches).

Why — the cohort method and not the seller’s

A de novo add-back is a forecast wearing an adjustment’s clothes. The honest version of the forecast is the platform’s own history: three cohorts, 14 branches, 41 branch-months of post-breakeven data. That history is good — breakeven arrives around month 11–13, reliably — and it still refutes the credited end-state. We underwrite the engine the room evidences, not the one the CIM describes.

4.2 Medicare Advantage “parity” — the contracts say the opposite

The seller restates all MA volume at Medicare FFS-equivalent yield, adding $6.9MM on the theory that “MA rates converge to FFS as plans compete for network adequacy” [SELLER]. The executed contract file (1.4.7) contains the refutation: two amendments signed in successive months of Q2 with the two national payors that carry 58% of the MA book convert them from episodic-legacy (~94% of FFS-equivalent) to per-visit schedules at 85–86%, effective at the next plan year. The remaining eleven MA plans average 81–83% today. Nothing in the room converges upward. The add-back is denied in full, and the signed step-down enters the calendar at §7 as a contracted event, not a scenario [VERIFIED].

4.3 Labor “normalization” — the mix reverts; the wage level is permanent

Contract labor has fallen from an 11.4% pandemic-era peak to 6.1% of clinical hours — real progress the platform earned with recruiting and scheduling work. The seller’s add-back extends the line all the way back to the pre-pandemic level (2.4%) and reprices the replaced hours at pre-pandemic wages, for $4.1MM. We allow normalization to the current market run-rate (~5%, worth $1.2MM) and nothing for wage reversion: W-2 clinical wages are up 31% over the period and the forward curve in the payroll cube is +4.0%, not zero. The remaining $2.9MM is a wish about the labor market, and the labor market has declined to cooperate for four consecutive years.

4.4 What the seller left out — cap accrual, EMR, and the small stack

Two deductions in the walk are ours, not corrections of the seller’s: the hospice aggregate-cap under-accrual (−$2.4MM), built from a PS&R-level rebuild of the cap model at §15.4, and capitalized EMR labor that recurs (−$1.6MM). Neither is exotic; both are the kind of item that surfaces when the accrual schedules are rebuilt rather than accepted. A third bucket (−$1.2MM, B3) aggregates items individually below materiality. Together they are the difference between “adjusted” and “underwriteable.”

4.5 The base of record — and the one upside held contingent

The underwriting base of record is ~$52.0MM (range $44–58MM). One possible upside is disclosed and held contingent, not merged: if the youngest de novo cohort holds its curve through month 30 — checkable for two of four branches before close — the base reads ~$54.7MM. Until then, $52.0MM governs every ratio, covenant and recovery figure in this package. The seller’s $78.4MM appears below only as the thing being tested.

7 · THE DOWNSIDE IS A CALENDAR, NOT A SENSITIVITY

The read

In most credits the downside is a scenario. In this one it is a schedule. 59.8% of revenue reprices by Federal Register rulemaking on fixed annual dates; 14.9% reprices at MA plan-year under amendments already signed; the hospice cap closes its year at every cap-year close whether anyone is watching or not. Of the seven events on the next twenty months, one is executed, two are published or accrued, three sit inside filed ranges — and one, the M+18 MA tranche-2 renewal, carries no number at all. The base case is not a guess about whether these land; it is an estimate of how hard.

7.3 One rule, three outcomes

The Y1 final rule publishes roughly sixty days before this loan closes. That accident of sequencing is worth using: the top $26MM of the recommended band funds only if the final lands at −2.5% or better, converting the single largest calendar risk from an underwriting assumption into an observed fact at pricing. Each 100bps of final-rule severity moves Y1 EBITDA ~$1.6MM.

Sensitivity, ranked. The same grid, moved one lever at a time, orders the risks — and the ordering is the point: the rule is worth more than the next three levers combined, and it is the only lever that resolves on a public schedule before funding.

And the regime, restated in dollars. The seller’s five-year average blends two different worlds; on this book, the difference between them is eleven million dollars of annual swing.

7.4 The open flank — MA tranche 2, January 2028

⚠ The one event without a number. The remaining 42% of the MA book — eleven plans, ~$30.4MM of revenue — reaches term 1 January 2028 with no negotiated successor rates and no floor. The tranche-1 precedent (85–86% per-visit) is the obvious anchor, and it anchors downward from the 81–94% the tail pays today. The downside case books this at 82%; the base holds current rates through Y2 and takes the step in Y3. The structure answers with the Y2 covenant step and the monitoring grid’s payor-mix line (§9a) rather than with optimism.

8 · RECOVERY

Why — recovery is built this way

There is no collateral floor in this credit, and the honest recovery analysis starts by saying so. The assets that matter — 76 Medicare provider agreements, state licenses, CON positions, referral relationships, a census of 12,384 patients — do not transfer outside a process. They move through CHOW, consents and continuity of care, or they evaporate. Recovery here is therefore a going-concern number first, a wind-down number second, and a forced-liquidation number only as a floor no one should plan to stand on.

8.1 Going concern — the number that matters

Stressed EBITDA of $34–40MM (the downside path with the harsh-end calendar and a Carrow leak) at distressed process multiples of 5.0–6.5× — where scaled home-based care has cleared in lender-led and 363 processes [EXTERNAL] — supports enterprise value of $170–260MM. Against the recommended $221MM attachment that is 0.77–1.18× coverage, a workout with a real menu: sale whole, sale by segment (hospice carries the multiple; personal care carries the option value), or amend-and-hold through a rule cycle. Against $360MM it is 0.47–0.72× — a restructuring with the lender underwater before professional fees.

8.3 The recoupment problem — the government collects first

Medicare and Medicaid hold recoupment and offset rights that operate against the receivable itself — overpayment determinations, cost-report settlements and audit extrapolations net out of current claims before any secured party sees a dollar, and most MA contracts import equivalent offset language. In a stress, the same events that cause the default (survey findings, billing disputes, a UPIC expansion) inflate the recoupment charge against the book. The wind-down stack below nets $9.4MM of identified offset exposure before the ordinary collectability haircut — and that figure is a Tier-1 diligence item (#34), not a settled fact.
