Part I — The Decision

1 · RECOMMENDATION

✔ PROCEED WITH CONDITIONS — at a first lien of $208–234MM (4.0–4.5× the corrected $52.0MM base), plus a $40MM springing RCF

Priced for the calendar: S+525, 1.00% floor, 98.5 OID, 1% amortization, 75% ECF sweep stepping to 50%/25% below 3.75×/3.00×, minimum liquidity $30MM, and the reporting package at §9a. The top $26MM of the band funds only if the CY27 home-health final rule (expected ~1 Nov 26 — before close) lands at −2.5% or better. All four Tier-1 conditions at §10 must be satisfied in full; any one unanswered is a decline at any attachment.

✘ DECLINE — at $300MM and above (5.75×+ through the corrected base; the FY27 trough turns the credit into a covenant negotiation in year one) and at the $360MM unitranche ask as framed (6.9× corrected · FCF negative through year two · going-concern recovery 47–72%)

The decline is arithmetic, not thematic. At the ask, cash interest of ~$34.7MM meets a trough-year EBITDA of $44.8MM that must also fund $9.7MM of capex, taxes, a working-capital build and an $18–24MM one-time stack. The sponsor’s own model (row 44) shows FY27 below its underwrite. Nothing about the asset changes between $234MM and $360MM — only the distance to the first bad November.

Why — the recommendation is built this way

The band is derived, not negotiated. $208MM is 4.0× the corrected base — the level at which the FY27 trough still produces positive FCF after full debt service with the one-time stack equity-funded. $234MM is 4.5× — the level at which the downside case (§6) still amortizes by FY29 under the sweep. The conditionality of the top $26MM exists because a single Federal Register document, published before close, moves the trough by ±$3–4MM (§7.3). Above $300MM no plausible final rule rescues the year-one math, which is why the decline threshold sits there and not at a rounder number.

4.1 De novo economics — credited to the trajectory, not the brochure

The seller’s single largest add-back credits all 14 de novo branches (and 3 recent tuck-ins) at a “mature-state” run-rate of $92K EBITDA per branch per month [SELLER]. The room contains monthly P&Ls for every branch since opening (1.6.2). No cohort is on a path to that number inside the credited window: the 2023 class — the oldest and best — reaches $51K at month 30; the 2024-H1 class tracks ~10% below it at the same age; the 2024-H2 class ~15% below that. We credit each branch to its own cohort curve, time-stamped, which is worth $3.2MM today [WINDROSE VIEW], with a further $2.7MM held contingent at §4.5 if the 2024-H2 class holds its curve through month 30 (verifiable pre-close for two of four branches).

Why — the cohort method and not the seller’s

A de novo add-back is a forecast wearing an adjustment’s clothes. The honest version of the forecast is the platform’s own history: three cohorts, 14 branches, 41 branch-months of post-breakeven data. That history is good — breakeven arrives around month 11–13, reliably — and it still refutes the credited end-state. We underwrite the engine the room evidences, not the one the CIM describes.

4.2 Medicare Advantage “parity” — the contracts say the opposite

The seller restates all MA volume at Medicare FFS-equivalent yield, adding $6.9MM on the theory that “MA rates converge to FFS as plans compete for network adequacy” [SELLER]. The executed contract file (1.4.7) contains the refutation: amendments signed 22 Apr 26 and 03 Jun 26 with the two national payors that carry 58% of the MA book convert them from episodic-legacy (~94% of FFS-equivalent) to per-visit schedules at 85–86%, effective 1 Jan 27. The remaining eleven MA plans average 81–83% today. Nothing in the room converges upward. The add-back is denied in full, and the signed step-down enters the calendar at §7 as a contracted event, not a scenario [VERIFIED].

4.3 Labor “normalization” — the mix reverts; the wage level is permanent

Contract labor has fallen from an 11.4% peak (Q3-22) to 6.1% of clinical hours — real progress the platform earned with recruiting and scheduling work. The seller’s add-back extends the line all the way back to the 2019 level (2.4%) and reprices the replaced hours at 2019 wages, for $4.1MM. We allow normalization to the current market run-rate (~5%, worth $1.2MM) and nothing for wage reversion: W-2 clinical wages are up 31% since 2019 and the forward curve in the payroll cube is +4.0%, not zero. The remaining $2.9MM is a wish about the labor market, and the labor market has declined to cooperate for four consecutive years.

4.4 What the seller left out — cap accrual, EMR, and the small stack

Two deductions in the walk are ours, not corrections of the seller’s: the hospice aggregate-cap under-accrual (−$2.4MM), built from a PS&R-level rebuild of the cap model at §15.4, and capitalized EMR labor that recurs (−$1.6MM). Neither is exotic; both are the kind of item that surfaces when the accrual schedules are rebuilt rather than accepted. A third bucket (−$1.2MM, B3) aggregates items individually below materiality. Together they are the difference between “adjusted” and “underwriteable.”

4.5 The base of record — and the one upside held contingent

The underwriting base of record is ~$52.0MM (range $44–58MM). One possible upside is disclosed and held contingent, not merged: if the 2024-H2 de novo cohort holds its curve through month 30 — checkable for two of four branches before close — the base reads ~$54.7MM. Until then, $52.0MM governs every ratio, covenant and recovery figure in this package. The seller’s $78.4MM appears below only as the thing being tested.

7 · THE DOWNSIDE IS A CALENDAR, NOT A SENSITIVITY

The read

In most credits the downside is a scenario. In this one it is a schedule. 59.8% of revenue reprices by Federal Register rulemaking on fixed annual dates; 14.9% reprices at MA plan-year under amendments already signed; the hospice cap closes its year every 31 October whether anyone is watching or not. Of the seven events on the next twenty months, one is executed, two are published or accrued, three sit inside filed ranges — and one, the January-2028 MA tranche-2 renewal, carries no number at all. The base case is not a guess about whether these land; it is an estimate of how hard.

7.3 One rule, three outcomes

The CY27 final rule publishes ~1 November 2026 — before this loan closes. That accident of sequencing is worth using: the top $26MM of the recommended band funds only if the final lands at −2.5% or better, converting the single largest calendar risk from an underwriting assumption into an observed fact at pricing. 7.4 The open flank — MA tranche 2, January 2028

⚠ The one event without a number. The remaining 42% of the MA book — eleven plans, ~$30.4MM of revenue — reaches term 1 January 2028 with no negotiated successor rates and no floor. The tranche-1 precedent (85–86% per-visit) is the obvious anchor, and it anchors downward from the 81–94% the tail pays today. The downside case books this at 82%; the base holds current rates through FY28 and takes the step in FY29. The structure answers with the FY28 covenant step and the monitoring grid’s payor-mix line (§9a) rather than with optimism.

7.5 What is not in the calendar — and deliberately so

Demand is not a downside driver in any case. Admissions have grown through every rate cycle on record; the demographic wave (§17, Exhibit X24) does the volume work. Nor is a compliance catastrophe modeled — not because the probability is zero, but because it is a gate, not a scenario: condition #34 exists precisely so that an extrapolated-overpayment exposure would surface before close and kill or reprice the deal on facts. The calendar models the risks that arrive on schedule; the conditions handle the ones that arrive by certified mail.

8 · RECOVERY

Why — recovery is built this way

There is no collateral floor in this credit, and the honest recovery analysis starts by saying so. The assets that matter — 76 Medicare provider agreements, state licenses, CON positions, referral relationships, a census of 12,384 patients — do not transfer outside a process. They move through CHOW, consents and continuity of care, or they evaporate. Recovery here is therefore a going-concern number first, a wind-down number second, and a forced-liquidation number only as a floor no one should plan to stand on.

8.1 Going concern — the number that matters

Stressed EBITDA of $34–40MM (the downside path with the harsh-end calendar and a Carrow leak) at distressed process multiples of 5.0–6.5× — where scaled home-based care has cleared in lender-led and 363 processes [EXTERNAL] — supports enterprise value of $170–260MM. Against the recommended $221MM attachment that is 0.77–1.18× coverage, a workout with a real menu: sale whole, sale by segment (hospice carries the multiple; personal care carries the option value), or amend-and-hold through a rule cycle. Against $360MM it is 0.47–0.72× — a restructuring with the lender underwater before professional fees.

8.3 The recoupment problem — the government collects first

Medicare and Medicaid hold recoupment and offset rights that operate against the receivable itself — overpayment determinations, cost-report settlements and audit extrapolations net out of current claims before any secured party sees a dollar, and most MA contracts import equivalent offset language. In a stress, the same events that cause the default (survey findings, billing disputes, a UPIC expansion) inflate the recoupment charge against the book. The wind-down stack below nets $9.4MM of identified offset exposure before the ordinary collectability haircut — and that figure is a Tier-1 diligence item (#34), not a settled fact.

14.1 The 36-month rule — seven provider numbers are travel-restricted

⚠ The transaction’s quietest gating item.
